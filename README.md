## Research Studies

### 1. Thermal Conductivity Enhancement in PDMS-Based Nanocomposites with 2D Fillers

**Undergraduate Thesis | Non-Equilibrium Molecular Dynamics (NEMD) | Thermal Interface Materials (TIMs)**

[**Explore Research Folder →**](PDMS_hBN/)

This study investigates the effects of **filler composition, volume fraction, and morphology** on the thermal conductivity of PDMS-based nanocomposites using non-equilibrium molecular dynamics simulations in LAMMPS. The investigated fillers include **hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN), graphene, boron nitride carbon (BNC), and carbon-doped h-BN**. Both united-atom (UA) and all-atom (OPLS-AA) force fields are considered for modeling PDMS.

The research aims to identify promising filler configurations for enhancing thermal transport in polymer-based thermal interface materials.

### 2. Vacancy-Engineered Thermal Transport in h-BN/Phosphorene Heterobilayers

**Research Project | Reverse Non-Equilibrium Molecular Dynamics (RNEMD) | Defect-Engineered Thermal Transport**

[**Explore Research Folder →**](2D%20material%20Heterobylayer%20new/)

This study investigates the effects of **vacancy concentration, defect configuration, and heat-flow direction** on the in-plane thermal conductivity of h-BN/black phosphorene heterobilayers using reverse non-equilibrium molecular dynamics (Müller–Plathe method) in LAMMPS.

The simulations examine isolated and clustered vacancy defects introduced into the h-BN layer, including **DB1, DB3, and DB5 configurations**, under zigzag and armchair heat transport. **Phonon density of states (PDOS)** analysis is employed to investigate how different vacancy concentrations and configurations influence the vibrational characteristics of the heterobilayer.

### 3. CO₂/N₂ Diffusion in Metal–Organic Frameworks

**Ongoing Research | Molecular Dynamics | Molecular Diffusion in Porous Materials**

[**Explore Research Folder →**](MOF_CO2_Difussion_New/)

This research investigates the **self-diffusion behavior of CO₂ under varying N₂ co-loading conditions** in metal–organic frameworks, including MOF-5 and metal-based MOF structures, using molecular dynamics simulations in LAMMPS.

The study evaluates the effects of **gas loading and framework pore characteristics** on molecular mobility. Mean-square displacement (MSD) analysis is used to estimate CO₂ self-diffusion coefficients and investigate directional differences in molecular transport.

## Undergraduate Engineering Projects


### 1. Comparative Analysis and Performance Evaluation of a Two-Wheeled Self-Balancing Robot using P, PI, and PID controllers

**Control Engineering | Dynamic Modeling | PID Control | Metaheuristic Optimization | MATLAB**

[**Explore Project Folder →**](Undergraduate%20Engineering%20Projects/Two-Wheeled%20Self-Balancing%20Robot/)

This project investigates the dynamic stability and control performance of a two-wheeled self-balancing robot through mathematical modeling and MATLAB-based simulation. The robot was modeled as an inverted pendulum using Euler–Lagrange equations, followed by linearization and state-space analysis.

A comparative evaluation of **P, PI, and PID controllers** was conducted using **Genetic Algorithm (GA), Clonal Selection Algorithm (CSA), Ziegler–Nichols tuning, and MATLAB Auto-Tuner**. Controller performance was assessed through rise time, settling time, overshoot, and undershoot.
